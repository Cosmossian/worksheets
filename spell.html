How the help works
  1. He hears the word, normal and slow, and can tap each part to hear just that part.
  2. The part he’s working on lights up, so he knows which sound to listen for.
  3. He taps a letter. Right letters stay in the box. A wrong letter wiggles, doesn’t stay, and its button turns red with an ✕ so he doesn’t pick it again. The red clears when he gets that letter.
  4. Help me is locked until he tries. It unlocks after the number of tries you pick (2 to start). Dots on the button show how many tries are left. If he taps it early, it tells him to try his best first and replays the sound.
  5. Tries only count if they’re real: after a wrong letter the keys pause for a second, taps that come too fast don’t count, and tapping a letter he already tried doesn’t count.
  6. Help goes one step at a time, with a new try needed between each: first it plays just that part slowly, then it narrows the letters down to 3 choices, then it shows a faint letter to copy. He still taps it himself.
  7. The “change word” button has to be held down for 2 seconds, so he can’t pop back here and read the word.
  8. At the end you’ll see a small summary of how many tries and helps he used.
  9. Quiet letters (like the e in “like”) are flagged, because he can’t hear them.
  10. At the end he sees the whole word big, to copy onto his paper.
