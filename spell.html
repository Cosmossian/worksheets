How the help works
  1. He hears the word, normal and slow, and can tap each part to hear just that part.
  2. The part he’s working on lights up, so he knows which sound to listen for.
  3. One-syllable words (like “Earth”) are split into sounds instead: ear · th. Letters that make one sound together are shaded as one band, so he can see that one sound can take 2 or 3 letters. Each sound plays a clue word (“er, like her”), since the computer voice can’t say a sound like “th” by itself.
  4. Your voice: tap Record next to the whole word or any part and say it. He’ll hear you instead of the computer voice, everywhere: the hear buttons, Say it slow and Help me. Recordings are saved on this device.
  5. He taps a letter. Right letters stay in the box. A wrong letter wiggles, doesn’t stay, and its button turns red with an ✕ so he doesn’t pick it again. The red clears when he gets that letter.
  6. Help me is locked until he tries. It unlocks after the number of tries you pick (2 to start). Dots on the button show how many tries are left. If he taps it early, it tells him to try his best first and replays the sound.
  7. Tries only count if they’re real: after a wrong letter the keys pause for a second, taps that come too fast don’t count, and tapping a letter he already tried doesn’t count.
  8. Help goes one step at a time, with a new try needed between each: first it plays just that part slowly, then it narrows the letters down to 3 gold choices, and he has to listen to all 3 before he can pick, then it shows a faint letter to copy. He still taps it himself.
  9. Every letter button has a speaker strip along the bottom. Tapping it says the letter and a word with its sound (“V, like van”) without picking it. During a normal run that’s optional.
  10. The “change word” button has to be held down for 2 seconds, so he can’t pop back here and read the word.
  11. Remember it: if he spells the same word again within the time you pick (30 minutes, 1 hour or 1 day), he’s told he spelled it recently and to remember it. Help me needs one extra try at every step and starts at the 3 gold letters, and the letters he struggled with last time get a ★. The finish screen says whether he did better than last time.
  12. At the end you’ll see a small summary of how many tries and helps he used.
  13. Words he needed the most help with show up under Practice again, with how long ago he spelled them. They drop off the list once he spells them with no trouble.
  14. Quiet letters (like the e in “like”) are flagged, because he can’t hear them.
  15. At the end he sees the whole word big, to copy onto his paper.
